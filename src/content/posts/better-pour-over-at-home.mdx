The single biggest upgrade to home coffee isn't a fancier machine — it's a
kitchen scale. Weigh the coffee, weigh the water, and suddenly yesterday's great
cup is repeatable.

Our house recipe is **1 gram of coffee to 16 grams of water**: so 20 g of coffee
to 320 g of water for a decent mug.

<Figure
  src="/img/pour-over-figure.svg"
  alt="A pour-over dripper over a glass carafe"
  caption="The whole kit: a dripper, a filter, a scale and a kettle."
/>

<Callout kind="tip" title="Start with the bloom">
Pour just enough water to wet all the grounds — about double the coffee's weight —
and wait 30 seconds before you carry on. It lets the coffee de-gas so the rest of
the brew is even.
</Callout>

Pour in slow circles, keep the water level steady, and aim to finish around the
three-minute mark.

![Pouring in slow, steady circles](../../assets/content-brew.svg)

If it's too fast, grind finer; too slow, grind coarser. That's the whole game.

<Callout kind="warning" title="One thing to avoid">
Don't let the water sit still for long between pours — a stalled brew over-extracts
the grounds at the edges and you'll taste it as bitterness.
</Callout>
